# Data Sources — Registry

Every external dataset this project has taken data from, with the identifier that
makes it citable, its licence, when it was retrieved, what was actually taken,
and how that is attributed in the product.

Downloaded datasets are kept on the external volume under
`/Volumes/MacieExternal/sources/`, indexed by `manifest.json` and generated by
`scripts/sourcesManifest.py`. That manifest is the machine-readable form of this
page: one record per dataset with its licence, status, what was taken, and a
sha256 per archive. Scripts resolve paths through `scripts/sources.py` (or
`scripts/paths.ts`) rather than hardcoding them, so an unmounted volume produces
a sentence rather than an ENOENT.

`archives/` holds immutable downloads and `work/` the derived artifacts, which
can be deleted and regenerated — the importers unpack on demand, so clearing
`work/` costs disk-time and nothing else.

One specific derived artifact worth naming: a transformer's output -- the
species/taxon records it produced from a source, before any merge or
cross-family dedupe decision -- is written as JSONL into that source's own
`work/<slug>/` via `record_transformed()` (`sources.py` / `paths.ts`), by
convention named `transformed.jsonl`. This makes a bulk import reviewable
or replayable without re-fetching or re-parsing the archive, and it needs
no separate registration: `sourcesManifest.py` already scans `work/`
recursively, so the file shows up in `manifest.json` the next time the
manifest is rebuilt. Like everything else under `work/`, it's overwritten
on each run, not kept as a history of every past one.

Passing metadata to `record_transformed()` writes a sidecar beside it
(`transformed.jsonl` → `transformed.meta.json`): the origin's own
identifiers (`source`, `sourceDatasetId` — e.g. a ChecklistBank dataset
key or GBIF `datasetKey`, distinct from our own `slug` — and `sourceUrl`),
where the original download lives (`archive: {path, sha256}`, omitted for
a live-REST source with no archive file), `fetchedAt` kept separate from
the auto-set `transformedAt` (a transform can be re-run against an
already-downloaded archive days later), and a fresh `runId` identifying
that batch — not any one species, which gets its own uuid from
`importwrite` at merge time, not here.

The point of writing `transformed.jsonl` rather than POSTing straight to
`importwrite` is that the two steps can then run separately — on
different machines, at different times, with the output reviewable in
between. `scripts/importTransformed.py` is the "apply" side: it reads the
file back (each record carrying enough placement —
`kingdom`/`phylum`/`class`/`order`/`family`/`genus` — to route it, since
one bulk source archive can span many families), groups it by family and
genus, and POSTs it to the one running `importwrite` instance. A
transformer that wants this split just writes records in that shape
instead of POSTing directly; nothing currently does, since every existing
transformer (`fetchFromChecklistBank.ts`, `fetchSpeciesFromApi.ts`) still
transforms and POSTs in one step.

This is the machine-checkable companion to
[`data-sources-attribution.md`](data-sources-attribution.md), which carries the
prose: required citation strings per source, the share-alike obligations, and the
licensing risks still open. **Nothing here is legal advice.**

Why a registry and not just the attribution doc: a source that is not written
down cannot be cited, and a source that is written down only in prose gets lost
the next time the tree is rebuilt. Each row here has a `sourcedFrom` value, a
sidecar field, or both, so the data can be traced back to the licence it was
taken under.

## Accepted archive formats

In preference order: **DwCA**, **ColDP**, **NEWICK**, **DOT**.

Two thirds of what we already hold is DwCA (28 of 39 archives are zips, and
essentially every zip is a DwCA or a Species File export of one), which is why
this ordering starts there: it is the one shape GBIF, ChecklistBank, Kew and the
Species Files all publish, and the one every importer here already reads.

Where a publisher offers both DwCA and ColDP, take **ColDP** — it is the same
model plus a per-name source attribution, and its absence is the recorded
weakness of what we hold: COL's DwCA export has 7 columns and no source field, so
every COL name is attributed to "COL" rather than to the ~160 checklists under it.
That is a provenance loss we cannot recover from a DwCA.

Being honest about the other two: **nothing in this repo reads NEWICK or DOT
yet.** No importer, no script, no sample file, and nothing on the volume. They are
the two formats a phylogeny arrives in, and a tree is genuinely something this
project has no way to hold — the structure is a taxonomy, not a phylogeny, and
`TaxonNode` has no field for branch lengths. So treat them as *preferred when a
source offers a choice*, not as *supported today*; taking one means writing the
reader first.

Archives are stored **zipped**, and the extension follows the publisher: 28 zip,
7 tgz, 3 gz, 1 xlsx. `tar.gz` appears only where that is all the publisher offers
(AntWeb, BioImages, CalPhotos, FishBase, Flickr, NMNH, msw3, Commons, USDA).

The Catalogue of Life is the single largest provenance in the tree and reached
that size in one day: Stage 1 took the species COL holds in families we already
had, and Stage 2 took everything else by scaffolding the families we lacked —
Animalia 5,472 → 10,601 families, Fungi 773 → 1,193, Chromista 703 → 1,002.
Its DwCA has **7 columns and no source field**, so every one of those 1.18M nodes
is attributed to "COL" rather than to the ~160 checklists underneath it. That is
a provenance loss we cannot recover from a DwCA, and it is the recorded
weakness of this source rather than a gap in this table.

`orthoptera species file` carries names, authorship, year, hierarchy and
distribution. It carries **no descriptions** and **no images** — its
`multimedia.txt` is empty. 371 of its 378 TDWG locality strings were mapped to our
continent vocabulary (`scripts/osfLocalities.py`); 7 were left unmapped on
purpose, so those species simply have no continent rather than a guessed one.

> The 620,509 nodes with no `sourcedFrom` are a real blind spot: "no source" and
> "source not recorded" are indistinguishable in the data. They are almost entirely
> the GBIF backbone import, which is public domain, but that is an assumption
> rather than a record. It is also a **falling** number — it was 669,002 before
> the Catalogue of Life import, because COL stamps provenance on everything it
> touches — so the blind spot is closing rather than being fixed.

Attribution owed:

- **CC0 (NMNH)** — none. That is why `Owner` being empty on all 453,486 NMNH rows
  is not a gap; the waiver is the point.
- **CC0 / public domain (Wikimedia)** — none.
- **CC BY / BY-SA (Wikimedia)** — author, licence and source. 184,542 entries carry
  an author and the panel prints `Wikimedia Commons · <author> · CC BY-SA 4.0`
  with the licence linked. Where the author is unknown the panel links the
  Commons `File:` page instead, which is where the author can be read.
- **BY-SA is share-alike.** Derivative reuse of those images must carry the same
  licence. Not yet stated anywhere user-facing — see open items.

### Conservation status

`iucnStatus` on a species node and the `rangeMaps[]` sidecar field come from
Wikidata (P141) and the IUCN range maps those point at. The IUCN Red List's own
terms are **not** CC-licensed; the maps are used as linked range references.

## Refused on licence

The EOL harvester republishes whatever a project publishes and does **not** filter
by licence. Of ten harvests assessed, seven were non-commercial.

`applyCommonsImages.py` refuses `-nc` and `-nd` outright, so a non-commercial
image cannot enter through the back door even though the 191,184 already in the
sidecar predate any licence check. Currently **0 NC/ND** entries are present.

## Open items

1. **No user-facing attribution notice.** `data-sources-attribution.md` has
   recommended a site-wide "Data sources & licences" page since 2026-07-06. The
   per-image credit now exists in the species panel; the dataset-level notice
   does not.
2. **BY-SA share-alike is not surfaced.** 190,647 entries carry a licence and
   68,000-odd are BY-SA, whose share-alike term is not stated anywhere the user
   can see it.
3. **Per-dataset GBIF licensing is unresolved.** 200,677 nodes are tagged `gbif`
   with no dataset DOI, so the licence of any individual record cannot be stated.
   GBIF aggregates datasets with incompatible terms including CC BY-NC.
4. **The four permission-based `websearch` sources** (FloraBase, Flora of China,
   NZPCN, SANBI) are still unresolved — ~8 species. See the attribution doc.
5. **620,509 nodes carry no `sourcedFrom`** and cannot be attributed from the
   data alone.

## iNaturalist (CC BY-NC 4.0, deliberate exception)

Fetched as a gap-filler for the 1.1M species with no common name. The sweep
collected 165,679 distinct names across all 5,651 pages; measured against the
tree it fills **10,485** species — 0.9%.

Names only. No images were taken from it: iNaturalist *photographs* are mostly
CC BY / CC BY-SA / public domain and mostly already represented via Wikimedia
Commons, whereas its *data* is CC BY-NC 4.0. So the one part that is freely
licensed is the part we did not need, and the part we did take is the part that
is NC.

Applied by `scripts/applyInatCommonNames.py`, which fills only empty
`commonName` slots — nothing from GBIF, Wikipedia or POWO is displaced, which is
why a crowdsourced source is tolerable here at all. Three names are skipped
rather than guessed: iNaturalist keys on the binomial alone, so a homonym gets
whichever name each taxon happens to have, and `Ficus variegata` turns up as
both a plant and a fig shell.

The ledger (`portal/data/inat-common-names.jsonl`, 31 MB, 175,496 rows) is
gitignored and lives on this machine rather than under `sources/`, since it is
regenerable by `scripts/fetchInatCommonNames.py` and is the only artefact here
that is not an archive.
